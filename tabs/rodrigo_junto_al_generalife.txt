Notes and Legend
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
"Joaquin Rodrigo is considered one of the most prominent guitar composers in the
classical guitar repertory. One piece in particular that he wrote, Junto al
Generalife, demonstrates the style of Spanish guitar music very well. The title
of this piece can be translated as "Next to Generalife". The Generalife is a
country home for the king of Granada. This was a place for the kings to vacation
and relax. The Generalife features an extravagant array of gardens. Rodrigo
really captivates this location in Junto al Generalife.
The piece starts off in a slow, smooth singing style. The main motivic idea is
the first five scale degrees of e minor. One major aspect of Spanish guitar
music is the use of scales. Rodrigo develops this scalar motif starting in
measure five and continues this melodic idea throughout the first section.
Rodrigo incorporates the different tone colors of the guitar by using "dolce"
and "metallico" to make the melodic content more interesting and contrasting.
These colors of the guitar are very important to the classical guitarists for
interpreting a melody. The next section of this piece also incorporates mostly
scalar melodic content. Rodrigo uses 'staccato', 'tenuto', and 'pizzicato' to
vary the melodic content." - Emanuel Estrada Yarce

1 - index finger                   p - thumb
2 - middle finger                  i - index finger (forefinger)
3 - ring finger                    m - middle finger
4 - little finger                  a - ring finger (annular)

CI     barre on the 1st fret
CIII   barre on the 3rd fret
CVI    barre on the 6th fret
[      barre

p      - piano - soft
f      - forte - loud
> > >  - (below the tab) decrescendo - becoming softer

met.       - metalique (ponticello) - near the bridge
dolce      - gently, sweetly
etouffe    - stifled, smothered - dampen the string immediately
             after plucking so the note doesn't sustain
pizz.      - muted form of plucking, with a shorter sustain - (place
             the right side, the side opposite the thumb, of palm of
             the right hand on the bridge, slightly resting on the
             bridge, pluck the strings with the thumb or fingers)
arp.       - arpeggiate
meno       - less
Tempo I    - Tempo primo - return to the original tempo

h      - hammer-on
p      - pull-off
g      - grace note (ornament) - extra note(s) immediately before the main note
         usually played as hammer-ons, pull-offs or slides
s      - staccato - detached, cut off - (see etouffe)
t      - tenuto - played firrmly, note held for at least its full length
>      - accented, marked, stressed
<12>   - natural (open) octave harmonic on the 12th fret
|-3-|  - triplet - 3 notes played in the time of 2 notes
