Notes and Legend
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Study Notes: "This study is for repeated notes, accents, and slurs. The
repeated note figure should be played moving from a light to an intense
touch in the right hand. Accents come out better by playing the following
more quietly rather than by playing the accented note more strongly. Bars
5 and 6 as well as bar 13 [also bars 32, 33 and 40] should be contrasted
with the others by the use of ponticello [right hand plucking near the bridge]
and staccato [notes played for less than their full value, so they sound
detached and cut off from each other]. Section D is p [piano - played quietly],
with the last quaver [eighth note] of each bar to be played staccato."

left hand fingering (LHF)      right hand fingering (RHF)
1 - index finger               p - thumb
2 - middle finger              i - index finger (forefinger)
3 - ring finger                m - middle finger
4 - little finger              a - ring finger (annular)

mezzo forte     mf  - medium loud
piano           p   - soft
forte           f   - loud
< < <           crescendo - growing louder
> > >           decrescendo - growing softer

Allegro       - at a brisk lively tempo
bpm           - beats per minute (quarter notes)
legato        - in a smooth flowing manner
dolce         - gently, sweetly
rit.          - ritenuto - slowing down, suddenly slower
a tempo       - at normal speed (after rit. - slowing down)
vibrare tutti - let all the notes vibrate
sempre        - always, still, continuing
F.            - Fermata - pause, notes held for longer than their value
breve         - note that has the time value of 2 whole notes (lasting 8 beats)
ten.          - tenuto - note played firmly for at least its full length
arp.          - arpeggiate (ripple upwards from the lowest note to the highest)

>       - accented, marked, stressed
h       - hammer-on
p       - pull-off
