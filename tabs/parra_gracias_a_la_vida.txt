Notes and Legend
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
This was written by Violeta in 1966, the year before she took her own life.
Violeta Parra's original recording - https://youtu.be/w67-hlaUSIs
Mercedes Sosa's classic recording - https://youtu.be/cIrGQD84F1g
Joan Baez recording - https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pz_7h2Ytq1k
Joan Baez & Mercedes Sosa recording - https://youtu.be/rMuTXcf3-6A
Another performance by Emmanuel Sowicz - https://youtu.be/lKdQkGGSy64

The grace notes in measure 55 don't seem to be played by Emmanuel Sowicz,
but are easily heard in the Stephanie Jones video.
The suggested total time for the piece is 2min 20 sec.
The volume expressions in measures 6 & 31 refer to the melody - the
accompanying notes are played softer.

left hand fingering (LHF) below tab
1 - index finger (forefinger)
2 - middle finger
3 - ring finger (annular)
4 - little finger (pinky)

=====  - length of note, note held, duration
=-=-=  - let the note ring, don't cut it short
/      - (below the tab) slide the finger up to the next note
\      - (below the tab) slide the finger down to the next note
/      - (between the notes)- side the note up without replaying it
'      - slight pause, breath mark
< < <  - crescendo - becoming louder
> > >  - decrescendo - becoming quieter
bpm    - beats per minute (dotted quarter notes)
C1     - barre on the 1st fret
c3     - part barre on the 3rd fret
C3     - barre on the 5th fret
C5     - barre on the 5th fret
dim.   - diminuendo - diminishing, becoming gradually softer
f      - forte
F.     - Fermata - pause, notes (or rests) held for longer than their value
TF.    - Triangular Fermata - short pause
g      - grace note (ornament) - extra note played just before the main note -
         following note is usually played as a hammer-on, pull-off or slide
h      - hammer-on
mf     - mezzo-forte - moderately loud (the melody)
molto  - much, greatly
p      - (below the tab) piano - soft, quietly
p      - (between the notes) pull-off
poco   - little, a little (poco a poco - little by little, gradually)
rall.  - rallentando - slowing down and becoming quieter
t      - tenuto - note held for at least it's full length, played firmly
tempo  - speed (a tempo - return to the earlier tempo)
<5>    - natural open (double octave) harmonic on the 5th fret
<19>   - natural open harmonic on the 19th fret (played with the right hand)

"Gracias a la vida, que me ha dado tanto
Me dio dos luceros, que cuando los abro
Perfecto distingo lo negro del blanco
Y en el alto cielo, su fondo estrellado
Y en las multitudes, el hombre que yo amo

Gracias a la vida, que me ha dado tanto
Me ha dado el oido, que en todo su ancho
Graba noche y dias, grillos y canarios
Martillos, turbinas, ladridos, chubascos
Y la voz tan tierna de mi bien amado

Gracias a la vida, que me ha dado tanto
Me ha dado el sonido y el abecedario
Con el, las palabras que pienso y declaro
Madre, amigo, hermano, y luz alumbrando
La ruta del alma del que estoy amando

Gracias a la vida, que me ha dado tanto
Me ha dado la marcha de mis pies cansados
Con ellos, anduve ciudades y charcos
Playas y desiertos, montanas y llanos
Y la casa tuya, tu calle y tu patio

Gracias a la vida, que me ha dado tanto
Me dio el corazon, que agita su marco
Cuando miro el fruto del cerebro humano
Cuando miro el bueno tan lejos del malo
Cuando miro el fondo de tus ojos claros

Gracias a la vida, que me ha dado tanto
Me ha dado la risa y me ha dado el llanto
Asi yo distingo dicha de quebranto
Los dos materiales que forman mi canto
Y el canto de ustedes que es el mismo canto
Y el canto de todos que es mi propio canto

Gracias a la vida..."

"Thank you to life, that has given me so much:
it gave me two bright stars, and when I open them
I can perfectly distinguish white from black
and in the high heavens, their starry depths
and among the multitudes, the man I love.

Thank you to life, that has given me so much:
it has given me my hearing, that in all its breadth
records night and day, crickets and canaries,
hammers, turbines, howls, and torrential rains,
and the tender voice of my beloved.

Thank you to life, that has given me so much:
it has given me sound, and the letters of the alphabet,
and with these the words that I think and declare:
mother, friend, brother, and light illuminating
the path of the soul that I am loving.

Thank you to life, that has given me so much:
it has given me the stride of my tired feet -
with them I have traversed cities and puddles,
beaches and deserts, mountains and plains,
your street, your house, and your doorstep.

Thank you to life, that has given me so much:
it gave me my heart, that shakes in its frame
when I look at the fruits of the human brain,
when I look at good, so distinct from evil,
when I look into the depths of your clear eyes.

Thank you to life, that has given me so much:
it has given me laughter and it has given me tears -
that's how I can tell joy from agony,
the two materials that form my song,
and your song, which is the same song
and everyone's song, which is my own song.

Thank you to life..."
