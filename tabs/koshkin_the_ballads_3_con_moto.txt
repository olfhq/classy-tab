===============================
rhythm :

h - 32nd note. For lenght/readability considerations, they're spaced like
    16th  notes, but indicated with an h under the tab.

fingerings :
when confusing or helpful, I indicated the left or right hand fingerings :
LEFT HAND                     |           RIGHT HAND
                              |
1 = index                     |           p = thumb
2 = middle finger             |           i = index
3 = ring finger               |           m = ring finger
4 = pinky                     |           a = pinky

h   - hammer-on
p   - pull-off
>   - accented note
^   - arpeggio
S   - slide
=   - note sustained
VI  - full barre on 6th fret
vi  - small barre on 6th fret
[vi - raised barre on 6th fret
h   - natural harmonic
rh  - nh played with right hand
ah  - artificial harmonic


an artificial harmonic is played with the right hand 12 frets above the fretted
note. Hover your index over said fret then pluck it with your ring finger.

a raised barre is the "opposite" of a small barre. Instead of fretting
the higher strings with your index, you make a "bridge" with it and
fret only the lower ones. If you can, angling your wrist helps.

nuances :

cresc      - louder
dim        - quieter
vivo       - lively, bright
risoluto   - resolutely, with marked accents
meno mosso - less quickly

dynamics :

.           - staccato
rit         - slowing down
sub         - suddenly
poco a poco - bit by bit

PERFORMANCE NOTES :

BAR 7  : starting the raised bar from the B string is easier
         left-hand wise. On the score it starts on E, which
         is better if you aren't confident with it yet, as it
         is easier to play

BAR 8  : most performances repeat the 8 first bars once.
         It's nice, just vary the nuances in the intro

BAR 10 : deviated from the score for playability.
         V barre is supposed to be played on the 3d
         beat and G to A hammer-on shhould be played
         with 2. I don't see a reason why you shouldn't
         make your life easier here

BAR 13 : lift second [ii (keeping the bass ringin)
         right after playing to be able to play the
         open B with ease. You can also do a II barre

BAR 15 : [ 7 ] indicates a 7-tuplet, so 7 notes in the
         space of a quarter note.
         This is a fancy way of telling you to do the
         glissando at a fixed speed. Do not play the D

BAR 19 : arpeggio strummed with i

BAR 24 : same
    25

BAR 25 : move the iii barre a string up to play the
         open D

BAR 27 : original fingering is
             3
         1 2 1

BAR 37 : this fingering makes to most sense to me.
         ON the score it's implied that you do the same
         as bar 36 but this seems easier

BAR 38 : sweep -0------- with a like an upstroke
               ---0-----
               -----5---
               -------4-

BAR 43 : nothing specified on the score but I like to strum
         the first 3 chords with i and the other 3 with p for
         a nice timber change

BAR 45 : the pull-offs should be carefully articulated
